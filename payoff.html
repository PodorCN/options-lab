Put–call parity check
StrikeCallPutCall ΔPut ΔC − PSe−qT − Ke−rT

The last two columns match for every strike: that is put–call parity, C − P = Se−qT − Ke−rT.

How the numbers are computed

CallC = Se−qTN(d1) − Ke−rTN(d2)
PutP = Ke−rTN(−d2) − Se−qTN(−d1)
whered1 = [ln(S/K) + (r − q + σ²/2)T] / (σ√T),  d2 = d1 − σ√T
P&LP&L(ST) = Σi ni · [ payoffi(ST) − Vi ]
P(win)ℙ(ST > x) = N( [ln(S/x) + (r − q − σ²/2)T] / (σ√T) )
  • C, P call and put price
  • S spot today; ST spot at expiry
  • K strike; x a breakeven level
  • T time to expiry (days / 365)
  • σ annualized volatility
  • r continuous risk-free rate
  • q continuous dividend yield
  • N(·) standard normal CDF
  • ni signed quantity of leg i (+ bought, − sold)
  • Vi BSM price of leg i today

European options. Premiums are not carried forward to expiry and dividends on the stock leg are ignored in P&L. The dashed curve re-prices every leg with BSM at the remaining time, same σ, r and q. Max profit, max loss and breakevens are solved exactly on the piecewise-linear expiry payoff, not read off a grid. P(profit) is under the risk-neutral lognormal distribution, not a real-world forecast. Θ is per calendar day, vega per 1 vol point, rho per 1% rate.

Source: Hull, Options, Futures, and Other Derivatives, ch. 11–12 (strategies, parity) and ch. 15 (BSM).
